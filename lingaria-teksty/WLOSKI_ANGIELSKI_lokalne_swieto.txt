The Harvest Festival in Willow Creek
Every October, my hometown of Willow Creek celebrates its Harvest Festival.
It is a tradition that is more than a hundred years old.
I moved away for work five years ago, but I never miss it.
This year I arrived on Friday evening, just in time for the opening parade.
Farmers drove their tractors, decorated with pumpkins and colorful flags, slowly down Main Street.
Behind them, the school band played so loudly that we could hardly hear each other.
On Saturday morning, my sister Hannah and I went to the pie contest at the town hall.
Every year, about thirty people bring their best pies, and a small jury tastes them all.
Hannah had baked an apple pie with cinnamon, although she had never entered a contest before.
She was so nervous that she almost dropped it on the way.
In the end she didn't win first prize, but she got a ribbon for the best crust.
She was very proud, and I must admit that her pie was delicious.
In the afternoon we walked around the market, where people sold honey, jam, and handmade candles.
I bought blackberry jam for my neighbor in the city, who feeds my cat when I travel.
The best part is always the big dinner in the park on Saturday night.
Neighbors who hardly talk to each other during the year sit together and share their food.
Someone always plays the guitar, and by the end of the night the whole park is singing.
Next year I want to come home a week earlier and help with the preparations.
