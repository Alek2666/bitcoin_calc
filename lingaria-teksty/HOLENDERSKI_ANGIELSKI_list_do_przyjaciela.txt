Greetings from the Harbor
Dear Clara,
I know I promised to write as soon as I had settled in, and I'm sorry it has taken me almost two months.
Between unpacking boxes, learning the ropes at my new job, and finding my way around, the weeks just disappeared.
I have to admit that moving to a town of only fifteen thousand people was a bigger leap than I had expected.
During my first week, I kept looking for the subway, only to remember that there isn't one.
Instead, everyone cycles or walks, so I bought a secondhand bike that squeaks terribly but gets me to work in ten minutes.
My apartment is on the top floor of an old brick building near the harbor.
It is smaller than my old place, yet the view of the water makes up for every square foot I gave up.
What surprised me most is how quickly people here get to know you.
My neighbor, Mrs. Delgado, knocked on my door on the second day with a freshly baked lemon cake.
Before I knew it, I had been invited to a barbecue, a book club, and a cleanup day at the beach.
I'm not sure I'm ready to say yes to everything, but I'd rather be too busy than lonely.
The job itself is going well, although it took me a while to get used to the slower pace.
At the office, nobody is in a rush, and meetings are often held over coffee rather than in the conference room.
My boss, who grew up here, insists that if you hurry in this town, you will only miss the best parts.
I used to think that was an excuse for laziness, but I'm starting to see her point.
Of course, there are things I miss, and you are at the top of the list.
I miss our Thursday evenings at the noodle place, and I even miss the noise of the traffic outside my old window.
The nearest movie theater is forty minutes away by car, so movie nights now happen on my couch.
The weather is another story: it has rained almost every other day since I arrived, and the locals just shrug.
"That's what keeps the gardens so green," they say, as if that settled the matter.
Last Saturday I finally explored the weekly farmers' market, where I bought tomatoes, cheese, and a ridiculous amount of honey.
I'm even thinking of joining the local choir, although you know perfectly well that I can't sing.
Anyway, I'm mostly writing to invite you to visit, so please write back soon.
If you could take a few days off in spring, I'd show you all my favorite spots.
Even Mrs. Delgado, who has never met you, has already promised to bake you a cake.
Warmly,
Nora
